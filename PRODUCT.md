# Al Rahma Hospital — HMS

**What it is.** The operational system a small private hospital in Qardho,
Puntland runs its day on: registration, queue, consultation, laboratory,
pharmacy, admission, billing and management reporting, in one place, replacing
paper registers.

**Who uses it.** Ten roles, almost all of them on their feet and in a hurry:
receptionists, doctors, nurses, pharmacist, laboratory staff, cashier, store
officer, HR officer, hospital management, and a System Owner who is the
proprietor or their nominated administrator.

**Where it runs.** On a single server inside the hospital, on a local network,
with no dependency on the internet. Power is intermittent, workstations are
inexpensive Windows machines in a bright room, and nobody on staff is an IT
specialist.

**What success looks like.** A receptionist registers a patient in under a
minute and finds a returning one on the first search. A pharmacist's stock
figure matches the shelf. A cashier's receipt is trusted in a dispute. The
owner opens one screen and sees the day's revenue without asking anyone.

**The primary job of the interface.** Disappear into the task. This is not a
product that is browsed; every screen is opened because somebody is mid-job
with a patient waiting.

## Assumptions, labelled

No interview was run for this file. It is inferred from the project brief, the
Phase 0 blueprint and two rounds of requirements the hospital supplied. Eleven
decisions (Q1–Q11 in the blueprint) remain open; four of them affect the
schema and are listed there.
